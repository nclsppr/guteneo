Asynchronous work is driven by durable references, current authorization and
conditional state transitions. A queue message is a request to inspect recorded
work, never a new approval. Scheduled maintenance recovers eligible work and
cleans bounded records; it does not grant authority to repeat a provider call.

The [dispatch lifecycle](/architecture/dispatch-lifecycle/) owns ordinary sending
states. [Secure distribution](/workflows/secure-distribution/) owns independent
render/deposit commitments. This page explains how their background work differs.

## Route the message to its actual job

The application consumer distinguishes ordinary dispatch references from secure
render and secure deposit work. Each path resolves the corresponding durable
record rather than accepting document bytes, recipient or cost supplied by the
queue as authority.

| Work | Durable responsibility | Retry boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Outbox publication | Publish already accepted work into the appropriate queue | Duplicate publication is possible; conditional claim prevents unconditional duplicate attempts |
| Dispatch consumer | Recheck current eligible command, approval, funding and fenced attempt | Unknown provider outcome requires reconciliation, never blind resubmission |
| Secure rendering | Produce a bounded batch for the recorded immutable source/policy | Resume the same preparation and validate results; no original fallback |
| Personal deposit | Build an independent encrypted copy before final activation | Same operation, no second acquisition/charge; recheck grant and fresh session at activation |
| Dead-letter handling | Record/reconcile exhausted or invalid work under its actual domain | Dead-letter presence is not permission to send again |

A lease bounds a worker's claim. Expiry means another worker may inspect/reconcile
that durable operation; it does not prove the previous external effect never
happened. Read/recovery retries and submission retries therefore have different
rules. Keep attempt identifiers and fingerprints through recovery.

## Scheduled recovery preserves the commitment order

Scheduled processing first reconciles durable callbacks before its identity gate,
then handles eligible outbox/leases, scanning, Studio work, secure jobs and cleanup
under their respective guards. This ordering lets already recorded provider
facts become durable state without converting unavailable identity or a new
configuration into approval for unrelated work.

Document/object/job maintenance uses bounded queries or batches and persistent
eligibility. Separate expired-session and HTTP-limit cleanup does not universally
use the same batch limits; do not describe every scheduled deletion as bounded.
Preserve cursor-based document work rather than replacing it with an unbounded
full-tenant scan. When adding
a job, define its authority snapshot, lease, idempotency key, maximum batch,
observable state, timeout and safe recovery before wiring it into the scheduler.

## Private original staging has its own cleanup fence

The disabled 26 October 2026 MCP App candidate stages fixed original-file parts
in private R2 objects with D1 receipts. The existing minute scheduler performs
this cleanup before identity/provider availability gates: an Auth0 or provider
outage must not indefinitely retain abandoned bytes. It adds no new scheduled
resource. Migration 0063 must precede publication of the code because this call
runs even with the App flag absent. The batch is at most two sessions and 80 parts
per tick.

A database-wide lease admits one App assembly at a time; it does not bound all
ordinary binary/inline requests or prove total isolate memory use. The same
bounded scheduler prioritizes an expired App-finalizer lease, fences its original
writer first, derives App state from the current original ledger and releases
logical capacity. Recovery may find an already registered document but never
reruns the content pipeline. Capacity recovery does not authorize early staging
or original deletion.

Cancel, expiry and revocation first fence writers. Cleanup may then remove only
the session's owned staging keys after checking writer/receipt state. A registered
or deduplicated original follows ordinary document policy and is never deleted as
staging. Retain the tombstone and key references needed to reconcile a late or
unknown write; neither a missing receipt nor a storage listing proves there was
no object write. D1/R2 separation makes uncertainty explicit.
Staging deletion waits for the original reservation expiry plus ninety seconds.
Pre-put refusal is settled and can release debt after cleanup; a missing object
for an actually uncertain put retains debt and immutable tombstone/key references
until settlement is established. It cannot authorize replay or inferred erasure.
Settled staging proven absent is excluded from future object maintenance; retained
metadata does not cause recurring R2 reads/deletes. Partial uncleaned/due and
state-leading finalizer indexes avoid rescanning settled history.

Monitor reserved staging debt and oldest unresolved age alongside actual
request/row/object/CPU/memory counts. Healthy cleanup is eventual; session expiry
is not an immediate deletion guarantee during outages. Do not resolve unknown
state by starting another upload, repeating the whole original or widening
retention/authority. The
[private original contract](https://github.com/nclsppr/guteneo/blob/main/docs/PRIVATE_DOCUMENT_UPLOAD.md)
owns exact bounds, migration, writer fencing and same-ID recovery, while
[MCP integration](/integrations/mcp/) explains the selected-local-original journey.
Local cleanup fixtures establish their controlled state transitions, not hosted
deletion latency, billing or real-client transfer.

## Retention is a graph of references

Ordinary PDF maintenance considers the existing ninety-day retention policy
and its protected-hosting/dependency exclusions. Dataset/template source expiry
is separately recorded, normally thirty days; active generation work defers
eligible dataset cleanup. Objects still referenced
by eligible work cannot be deleted as “orphans” merely because a first listing
failed to show them. Session expiry and storage cleanup are separate effects.

Secure objects add independent shared, personal and pending-deposit lifetimes.
An acquired personal copy cannot depend on continued existence of its sender's
workspace objects. A still-authorized pending copy must survive resumable work;
failed/expired invisible staging can be cleaned under its own bounded rules.

| Event | What changes | What does not follow |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared access revoked | Future authorized deliveries stop | No refund, recalled pixels, source erasure or acquired-copy deletion |
| Personal copy deleted/expired | Owner loses reading/export; eligible objects can purge | No workspace-source deletion or removal of previous exports |
| Detailed access activity reaches retention | Bounded purge of page/text/download details | Immutable approval, association, charge and acquisition receipts remain separate |
| Membership removed | Workspace authority ends | Acquired personal copies are not erased |
| Orphan candidate found | Recheck references/eligibility before deletion | A storage listing alone grants no deletion authority |

## Recovery must survive storage/database separation

D1 and R2 do not form one distributed transaction. Rendering/copying writes
invisible objects first; conditional database activation establishes a usable
result. Recheck authority after storage waits and before final response or
activation. Failed verification or durable-audit insertion must deny delivery.

Backups/restoration must preserve object hashes, key versions, revocations,
retention commitments, reservations and outbox order. Restoring old records
without matching current revocation/settlement knowledge can reopen access or
repeat work. Local restoration tests exercise their controlled dataset; hosted
restoration and key recovery require their own qualified rehearsal.

## Observe facts without leaking payloads

Use durable job identifiers, fixed error codes, states, batch progress and bounded
counts. Do not log content, recipients, tokens, signed URLs or encryption secrets.
Show `preparing`, waiting, failed and unknown as distinct states. Closing a tab
need not cancel accepted work, and a progress timeout is not successful delivery.

When work stalls, inspect the same operation and its lease/evidence before making
a new command. [Troubleshooting](/engineering/troubleshooting/) separates safe
reads from explicit recovery and operator escalation. [Provider maintenance](/engineering/provider-maintenance/)
explains why an old callback or tariff cannot qualify a new submission.
