Guteneo's workflows combine persisted states with clocks, leases, current
permissions and immutable receipts. A dispatch can remain `prepared` while its
approval expires. An upload can be `complete` while its PDF remains quarantined.
A delivered fax can still hold credit until qualified usage evidence settles it.
Reading only a `status` enum misses these distinctions.

The November 2026 implementation makes the highest-risk transition decisions
explicit in small TypeScript modules, while keeping D1 conditional writes and
constraints authoritative. The repository inventory records 113 app-owned
lifecycle groups. Its execution ledger distinguishes changed code from existing
simple mechanisms retained after review. That count does not represent 113 new
classes, reducers or minimal mathematical state machines.

Local tests establish the stated service and browser behavior. They do not
establish hosted-provider delivery, native-device behavior or production
activation. The repository's dated execution record owns the exact validation
results and limits.

## Read a machine as state, events, guards and effects

For each lifecycle, identify:

- **State:** persisted status and the fields that refine it, including lease
  ownership, progress, revision, row presence and expiry.
- **Events:** an explicit human command, a verified provider fact, a scheduled
  recovery step, a clock boundary or an asynchronous response.
- **Guards:** current actor and workspace authority, immutable content/price,
  source readiness, available budget and exact predecessor or lease.
- **Effects:** the atomic records to write and any storage, queue or provider
  operation that follows. Give an irreversible invocation its own boundary.
- **Recovery:** which same-operation reads or resumptions are allowed, which
  command needs fresh authority, and which uncertain outcome must stop sending.

A state name and a complete transition contract are different levels of
explicitness. Existing SQL constraints, conditional writes and event ranks
already express parts of these machines. The remaining work is to make their
composition reviewable and verify that code, persistence and clients agree.

## Treat dispatch as several coordinated machines

| Axis | Why it needs its own contract |
| --- | --- |
| Dispatch status | Describes preparation, queue ownership and projected provider facts |
| Approval validity | Can change with time, permissions or policy while the dispatch status stays prepared |
| Attempt ownership | A conditional claim and durable invocation marker determine whether a provider call is permitted |
| Queue publication | A lost acknowledgement permits duplicate publication, which must not duplicate the provider operation |
| Financial finality | Delivery and accepted transport do not by themselves settle actual-usage fax cost |
| Receipt projection | Durable callback ingestion can succeed before its event is linked to a dispatch |

The [dispatch figure](/architecture/dispatch-lifecycle/) shows the atomic acceptance
boundary. Its drawers correspond to one commitment: queue eligibility, reservation
and outbox insertion. The acceptance transaction remains authoritative even when
clients or workers use explicit transition helpers.

Email delivery can later acquire bounce or complaint facts. A callback can skip
intermediate visible statuses; a late verified delivery can supersede failure
under the existing channel-specific rank rules. A diagram must therefore avoid
treating every displayed success or failure as an absorbing state.

## Preserve the different recovery contracts

A lease expiration grants permission to inspect the same operation under its
current rules. It does not prove that an earlier effect failed.

| Interrupted operation | Recovery that preserves its contract |
| --- | --- |
| Dispatch after possible provider invocation | Read/reconcile the same attempt; an expired submitting lease becomes unknown, never a fresh send |
| Generation record below its attempt bound | Reclaim the expired lease, verify/reuse a retained artifact, recheck authority and finish the same record |
| Multipart part or final registration | Establish an exact stored receipt before advancing; unknown storage or pipeline outcome does not permit blind retransmission |
| Personal deposit with expired authentication | Leave unfinished work preparing, release ownership and require a fresh eligible session; revoked/expired source grant or failed frozen deposit eligibility aborts it |
| Client response from an old workspace or view | Discard it using the current identity/path/revision; cancellation alone does not grant publication authority |
| Provider-reference refresh | Recheck binding and exact predecessor; reconcile a retained applying plan before replacing immutable revisions |
| Logical document revocation | Deny future access immediately under current guards; physical erasure and an independent acquired copy follow their own lifecycles |

Strong document authentication has operation-specific freshness limits. The
verified-account policy uses the current eligible unexpired session under its own
rules. Recipient association likewise follows the configured independent or
verified-email policy. Preserve both branches when modeling access; see the
[secure workflow](/workflows/secure-distribution/) for their contract.

## Keep decision models small and integrated

The dispatch module names review and recovery decisions, bounded approval
deadlines, callback ranks, attempt outcomes and lease expiration. DomainService
uses those decisions inside its existing transaction and provider boundaries.
Quote, approval, membership and money remain independently checked axes.

Upload models distinguish writer ownership, exact stored receipts, finalization
and observation of an unknown result. Final private-upload completion checks the
same nonpurged document in SQL; a successful earlier read cannot authorize a
later completion after purge. Secure preparation and personal deposit models
preserve paid progress across an expired session while releasing ownership.
Revoked source authority still aborts unfinished copying.

Studio models cover dataset recovery, mapping validation, record command and
lease decisions, aggregate job settlement and per-entry distribution association.
Writes recheck the live membership or OAuth binding and exact source/predecessor,
including after analysis or rendering. Postal models preserve exact reviewed
preflight and transfer identities; an uncertain transfer remains observation of
the same operation, never a new provider submission.

These modules have no parallel persisted state store and introduce no statechart
dependency. Existing one-use capabilities, provider refresh plans, process locks
and bounded caches retain their existing mechanisms where those are already
simple and explicit.

## Give browser responses current-view authority

A shared current-view guard binds a mounted controller to its complete identity,
exact location and navigation generation. Check it after every asynchronous
boundary before another write, state publication or navigation. Leaving and
returning to the same URL still invalidates an old continuation; a cancelled
navigation blocked by an unsaved-changes guard does not invalidate the view.

Postal and secure-write controllers use explicit ready/writing/read-required/
reading states and stable same-operation keys. Source-copy recovery retains its
receipt without automatically preparing or approving a communication. Studio AI
adoption retains the completed mapping step if draft creation fails, so recovery
performs only the unfinished step. These guards complement current server
authority and cannot establish human consent.

## Define durable completion before writing it

Campaign completion is operational and financial closure of frozen, nonempty,
immutable membership. Migration 0075 derives eligible campaigns from closed
dispatches, finished invocation evidence and settled reservations. Postal
handover closes postal execution; it does not prove recipient receipt. Late
verified provider facts can still advance the existing delivery projection.

Dead-letter resolution is a separate diagnostic transition. A current browser
administrator deliberately resolves one tenant-scoped incident only after its
original dispatch is durably closed. Migration 0076 requires the matching audit
receipt and preserves the incident identity and first resolution timestamp.
Resolution has no dispatch, quota, credit, outbox or provider effect.

Account deletion is a separate, private operator workflow with a frozen reviewed
plan and verified checkpoints. It must block unsafe workspace closure and
unresolved liabilities, revoke authentication before erasure, and retain only
reviewed necessary evidence. Processing remains processing through uncertain
storage, identity or notification evidence. A disabled account is not completed
deletion. The account-deletion contract documents eligibility, external
verification and the default-off production gate.

## Verify the effect boundary

The [testing guide](/engineering/testing/#validate-transitions-through-the-persistence-boundary)
explains how to compare a transition contract with actual migrated D1 and service
behavior. A test that repeats a transition table does not establish persistence,
concurrency or provider effects. Characterize concurrent confirmation, duplicate
queue delivery, interrupted invocation, callback order, revoked authority during
awaits, expiry and delivered-but-unsettled usage through the real service.

## Maintain the contract with its implementation

Keep the detailed inventory, source/test anchors and dated audit evidence in the
repository. This page is the curated architectural explanation. Update the owning
topic guide, feature registry, transition contract and relevant tests together
when behavior changes; follow the [contribution workflow](/engineering/contributing/).

An explicit transition model improves reviewability only when its effects and
failure paths still agree with persistence. Report local, hosted, authenticated
and real-delivery proof separately after each implementation step.
