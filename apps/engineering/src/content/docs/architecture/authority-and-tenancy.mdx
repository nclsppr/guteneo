Identity answers who is connected. Membership selects the current workspace.
Permissions determine eligible operations. Approval authorizes one exact
communication. Provider and financial gates determine whether that authorized
operation can proceed. None of these layers replaces another.

## Resolve the tenant on the server

Private lookups, unique keys and relations are workspace-scoped. The authenticated
membership or recorded OAuth connection determines the workspace; an arbitrary
organization field/header cannot switch it. A person may belong to several
workspaces with different rights.

Changes to membership, connection binding or revocation must be checked at action
time. A previously issued token or a previously loaded page is not proof that the
same authority still exists. Asynchronous suggestions and document reads also
recheck their relevant resource/connection state before returning sensitive data
or committing writes.

## Four human roles

| Role | Effective responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Administrator | Workspace/member/channel/billing administration and preparation/approval; delegation requires the browser |
| Supervisor | Preparation plus independent approval/reporting options granted by an administrator |
| Operator | Preparation for authorized review; no confirmation, rejection or cancellation authority |
| Observer | Read ordinary operational content without mutation |

The wire role identifiers remain `admin`, `supervisor`, `member` and `viewer`.
Supervisor options do not grant membership management, billing or delegation.
Observers are not a private-folder role: creator-private Studio documents have
separate resource checks. Platform oversight is also separate from workspace
administration and is not an assistant or membership role.

## Clients retain their own boundaries

Browser mutations require a valid session, same origin and CSRF. Native sessions
use the mobile API and their own credential scheme; the companion can prepare
within current rights but final approval/confirmation remains in authenticated
browser review. A native administrator does not thereby gain a native approve
endpoint.

OAuth-connected assistants receive the intersection of consented scopes, current
membership, connection binding and resource access. Host execution permission or
a conversational “yes” is not fresh human consent in guteneo. Tool annotations
explain effects to the host; they grant no server permission.

## Optional delegation is explicit and bounded

Standard human review is the default. Only an authenticated browser administrator
may grant one OAuth connection an expiring expert mandate with selected channels,
per-dispatch/daily monetary limits and count limits. The assistant cannot enable,
extend or enlarge that mandate. The server records delegated authority distinctly
from human approval.

Review tokens bind the exact dispatch/document, current connection and policy,
quote, bounds and completed page provision. Replacing a token invalidates the
previous one. A token proves recent provision under server checks, not the
model's understanding of document content.

Postal draft transfer has its own delegated authority and provider-transfer
consent. Preparation transfer and final sending are separate side effects.
Preserve host confirmations, OAuth scopes and provider gates in addition to the
mandate; do not suppress one layer because another layer passed.

## Preserve resource privacy through the whole operation

A private Studio PDF stays creator-private in the library. A currently authorized
approver can receive access to the exact PDF in its prepared-dispatch context;
that access neither exposes unrelated documents nor survives lost approval rights.
Changing the underlying version invalidates the old review context.
