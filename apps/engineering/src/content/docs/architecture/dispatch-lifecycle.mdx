A dispatch is one communication with an immutable preparation fingerprint. Its
state is persisted by the shared domain service, while approval is a separate
record. There is no general `approved` dispatch status to substitute for checking
that approval record and its current authority.

## Prepare the version that will be reviewed

Preparation validates the actor, document, sender, recipient, channel, options
and financial bounds. It records a `prepared` dispatch with a fingerprint over
its exact normalized content and options. The browser review must show that
version, not a later editable preview.

A changed document, recipient, sender, option or quote is a changed commitment.
It needs the appropriate new preparation/review. An idempotency key identifies
one request; reusing it with different content is a conflict rather than a way
to update an existing approved command.

## Approval is authority for exact content

The standard path uses an authenticated human browser session with Origin/CSRF
checks. A connected assistant cannot assert that a person reviewed a document.
An optional expert mandate is a separate administrator-granted, bounded and
expiring authority; its approvals are recorded as delegated.

A valid approval must still match the fingerprint, current permissions, document
readiness, quote and other gates when acceptance is requested. Merely opening a
review link or receiving a quote creates no approval.

The fifteen-minute approval cap starts at its recorded creation time. Validation
may await storage or quote checks, but uses that same instant for the cap;
session, quote, strong-authentication or expert-review limits can expire sooner.
The SQL creation/update guards remain authoritative.

<GutenbergNote pose="review" title="Proof first, stamp second">
  An approval seals one exact proof. Change its pages, destination or price and
  it needs another review; the old stamp cannot authorize the new version.
</GutenbergNote>

## Acceptance makes one atomic commitment

Confirmation performs the transition to `queued` only if all required guards
still hold. In the same atomic commitment it reserves quota/credit and inserts
outbox work. A response lost after acceptance must not lead the client to create
a second dispatch with a new key: first reread the original operation.

| State or record | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `prepared` | Exact communication is available for review; an approval may be recorded separately |
| Approval record | Human or delegated authority bound to the immutable version and current policy |
| `queued` | Acceptance and reservation committed; asynchronous work is eligible |
| `submitting` | A consumer has conditionally claimed one attempt |
| `submission_unknown` | The provider outcome cannot safely be classified; do not automatically resend |
| `accepted` | The provider accepted submission; delivery is not yet established |
| `delivered` | A verified delivery fact has been applied |
| `failed`, `cancelled` | Failure/cancellation under the documented state constraints |
| `printed`, `handed_to_post`, `bounced`, `complained` | Channel-specific later facts, interpreted without pretending that all channels share identical delivery semantics |

## Queue delivery can repeat; provider attempts cannot be blind

Outbox publication is recoverable and queue messages may be delivered more than
once. The consumer's conditional claim prevents an already claimed dispatch from
being submitted again simply because the same message arrived twice.

If a submitting lease expires without a trustworthy outcome, the dispatch becomes
unknown rather than returning to the queue. Cancellation can stop eligible
prepared/queued work, but cannot promise to retract an external transmission.
Provider-specific reconciliation is needed once submission may have happened.

## Delivery and settlement are different facts

Provider callbacks are authenticated, stored durably and projected idempotently.
Acceptance does not prove receipt. Postal handover does not prove final delivery;
email complaint/bounce facts may arrive later.

For fax usage, a terminal delivery alone does not establish the final financial
settlement. Qualified usage evidence must match the tenant, attempt, quote and
provider reference before the bounded customer debit and reserve release. A
customer estimate or supplier diagnostic field is not that proof.

For implementation entry points, read `packages/domain/src/index.ts`, the related
migrations and provider event adapters. Integrations should follow the
[REST sequence](/integrations/rest-api/) or [MCP authority model](/integrations/mcp/)
without building another acceptance path.
